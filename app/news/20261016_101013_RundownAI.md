###### META

#### 🧠 _**[Meta gives the West its best open-weight model](https://research.meta.ai/blog/muse-spark-open)**_

View image: (https://media.beehiiv.com/cdn-cgi/image/fit=scale-down,format=auto,onerror=redirect,quality=80/uploads/asset/file/c305cc17-d9d6-44f7-bff9-ec1218ed30ca/4d0e37a9-36d9-4432-93a2-868aff5513cf.jpg?t=1792115803)
Caption: Image source: Mark Zuckerberg on X

**The Rundown: **Meta just [released](https://research.meta.ai/blog/muse-spark-open) Muse Spark Open, an open-weight, 816B-parameter multimodal model built for coding and agentic work, and worked with Apple so that it could run locally on Mac Studios — a first for a model in this size class, Meta says.

**The details: **

* Two M5 Ultra Mac Studios with 1TB combined memory can run Spark Open via Apple’s updated MLX toolkit, with Meta saying the model shipped iOS app fixes and edited video in DaVinci Resolve without the cloud.

* On Artificial Analysis’ Intelligence Index, it sits at 39, ahead of the West’s open-weight models but behind China’s GLM 5.3 (45), Kimi K3 (44), and Qwen3.8 (40).

* Spark Open either matches or tops open rivals on multimodal and knowledge tasks, but Chinese models still code better, with Kimi, GLM, and Qwen all ahead.

* It’s the third major Western open launch this month, after Reflection AI’s Beam and Mistral’s 1T-parameter Le Chonk, whose weights arrive Oct. 27.

**Why it matters: **Meta built its AI rep on open Llama models before going closed. Now it’s playing both sides with Wonder at the frontier and Spark Open giving devs serious AI they can run on their hardware. China still leads the open-model race, but Meta’s return puts an American heavyweight back in contention.

#### 🔎** **[_**My AI walks my company’s factory floor **_](https://app.therundown.ai/community/posts/0ab88067-320e-49ee-b12c-fa87e2da7d39)

**Rowan:** At an in-person company, the CEO can walk the factory floor. You watch how the company actually runs, catch errors in real time, spot slow handoffs, and identify processes worth automating. The best operators do it religiously.

The problem is that The Rundown is fully remote and has just over 20 people.

There is no floor to walk. So every Friday, I do what I call a digital factory inspection: I pick random important things across the company (support queries, newsletter posts, specific finance items, etc.) and review each one with the same lens. What’s slow? Which handoffs could disappear? What should be automated?

This week, I handed the entire prep to AI. A scheduled Claude task now runs every Friday before I wake up, and it:

1. Picks the random areas to inspect (randomness matters so nothing gets gamed)

2. Pre-gathers the samples, links, and data for each area into a report in my Notion

3. Checks last week’s findings against Slack to verify they actually got fixed before I move on

If you run a remote company (or any team, or honestly just your own workload), steal this. I put [the exact scheduled task prompt in our Workflow Hub](https://app.therundown.ai/community/posts/0ab88067-320e-49ee-b12c-fa87e2da7d39), ready to adapt to your tools in about 15 minutes.

It’s unglamorous plumbing, but it’s some of the highest-leverage 30 minutes of my week.

###### AI RESEARCH

#### 👨🏻‍⚖️ [_**Report: Harvard maps AI’s gender divide**_](https://shorensteincenter.org/resource/women-ai/)

View image: (https://media.beehiiv.com/cdn-cgi/image/fit=scale-down,format=auto,onerror=redirect,quality=80/uploads/asset/file/5900b6a3-b0cf-4394-8d42-92def0abb0bf/e0113065-6f47-48aa-acd5-020e8931baa0.png?t=1792121947)
Caption: Image source: ==Harvard Kennedy School’s Shorenstein Center==

**The Rundown: **Harvard researchers just [found](https://shorensteincenter.org/resource/women-ai/) consistent gender gaps in how Americans use AI, get support for it, understand it, and appear in coverage of it, drawing on a survey of 5,400+ adults, browsing data, and 78K news articles.

**The details: **

* 29% of women said they’d never used AI vs. 22% of men, though browsing data showed no real gap in trying it, only in how much time users spent on it.

* At work, 64% of employed women reported no AI training vs. 49% of men, with the study linking training and encouragement to higher use and confidence for both.

* Men answered more AI questions correctly while women chose “not sure” more often, though more men also claimed to recognize made-up AI figures.

* AI headlines named men 14.6x as often as women, a gap the researchers partly tie to male-led companies, though the gap also held among quoted experts.

**Why it matters: **This is one of the broadest looks yet at how AI’s gender gaps are taking shape. Access looks nearly equal today, but the real divide shows up in training, confidence, and who gets heard. As AI labs pitch superintelligence for everyone, these gaps show how far that promise still has to go.

###### COMMUNITY AI WORKFLOW OF THE DAY

**▸ Tony used AI agents to build and refine a word puzzle**

==Today’s workflow comes from reader ====**Tony Surfwell**====:==

“I’m not a professional developer, but I built Sujido, a live word puzzle, with multiple AI agents. For months, they gave me bloat, lost context, and slop, and neither the models nor I could untangle what had been decided or why. I eventually settled on two agents: one Claude instance that plans and reviews, and another that builds. I imposed rules on both, one failure at a time.

None of these rules would surprise a pro engineering team, but nobody handed them to me. That’s the lesson I learned: agents won’t bring the discipline. You have to impose it.

When I showed the first game revision to strangers, they said that the first half was ‘boring’ and the second half was novel. It was hard news to hear about my baby, but I took the feedback and cut the game in half. That required surgery on code tangled across both halves, followed by a rebuild under the rules below.

Before the rebuild, a separate AI audit graded the codebase B−. Afterward, it graded it A−. The site shrank by 97.5%, and every serious finding was fixed and locked in by an automated test. The models didn’t change drastically, but my operating rules did.”

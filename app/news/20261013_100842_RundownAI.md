###### META

#### 🍉 **[_Meta’s Muse Wonder lands at the frontier_](https://research.meta.ai/blog/muse-wonder)**

View image: (https://media.beehiiv.com/cdn-cgi/image/fit=scale-down,format=auto,onerror=redirect,quality=80/uploads/asset/file/bce02aab-3d94-490e-9da0-6b8afe315dd9/wonder.png?t=1791827222)
Caption: Image source: Meta

**The Rundown: **Meta Superintelligence Labs just [released](https://research.meta.ai/blog/muse-wonder) its long-anticipated Muse Wonder (Watermelon) and Muse Wonder Full, two powerful new AI models, with the full version landing #2 in Artificial Analysis’ intelligence rankings behind Anthropic’s Opus 5.5.

**The details: **

* Gemini 4 Argon, GPT-6 Astra, and Claude Fable 5.1 all hit 53 on AA’s Intelligence Index, putting Wonder Full three points clear of the pack and two behind Opus 5.5.

* In tests, Wonder Full passes Opus 5.5 and Astra at legal work, long coding, and memory, with Opus leading on health, computer use, and a few coding areas.

* Wonder Full is limited to Muse Code plans at $50 or $200/mo, while the standard model is live on Meta’s API and rolling out to paid Muse app subscribers.

* Both models are closed, unlike Meta’s open Llama era, with Alexandr Wang saying API prices will sit “meaningfully below” OAI and Anthropic’s top models.

**Why it matters: **Meta just forced its way into the frontier, which has been a two-horse race between OpenAI and Anthropic so far. The climb has been impressive, but the scarier part for rivals is everything around the model — the data centers, billions of users, and a hit agent app in Muse that are hard qualities for other labs to replicate.

###### AI & GLOBAL USAGE 

#### 🌎 **[_Andrej Karpathy sketches AI’s ‘sharp funnel’_](https://x.com/karpathy/status/2109361546505966046?s=20)**

View image: (https://media.beehiiv.com/cdn-cgi/image/fit=scale-down,format=auto,onerror=redirect,quality=80/uploads/asset/file/3ea01c9b-d2b5-4429-a42d-7beb71691df4/ChatGPT_Image_Oct_12__2026__06_20_08_PM.png?t=1791847220)
Caption: Image source: Andrej Karpathy (@karpathy on X)

**The Rundown: **OpenAI co-founder Andrej Karpathy just [revisited](https://x.com/karpathy/status/2109361546505966046?s=20) his April post on how people judge AI, saying it has widened into a “sharp funnel” where only a tiny fraction sees what frontier AI is capable of while the majority uses lower-quality free options. 

**The details:**

* Karpathy estimates that ~5000 people have access to true frontier AI inside labs, with capabilities like weeks-long agent swarms or solving open math problems. 

* He said the funnel’s wide end holds nearly 6B people (3/4 of the world) who’ve hardly used AI, plus 1-2B users who only utilize free chatbots for quick answers.

* Karpathy traces the split to access tiers, problem size, and models that excel where answers are checkable and lucrative, like code and math.

* The researcher called it the “weirdness of the moment,” with most people still underestimating AI while a select few feel “the curve going vertical.”

**Why it matters: **If you’ve talked AI with anyone outside the bubble, you’ve likely felt the weirdness Karpathy notes — a huge disconnect between what people think the tech can do and what models already handle. Consider internal models, and the same disconnect plays out between the public, regulators, and the insiders feeling the acceleration.

**The Rundown:** In this guide, you’ll use Meta’s new Muse Wonder to turn a workshop brief into an agenda and budget. Then you’ll change the requirements and check whether it catches a plan that no longer works.

**Step-by-step:**

1. Open [Muse Code](https://dev.meta.ai/products/muse-code), sign in, and select Wonder. Check your access and billing first. The consumer Muse app has a separate rollout

2. Paste the guide’s [workshop brief](https://app.therundown.ai/guides/muse-wonder-guide), including the fixed times and activity lengths. Ask for a draft agenda in the conversation, with no bookings or messages

3. Check the first agenda and budget. All six activities should fit before noon, and the planned cost should be $990 for 24 people

4. In the same conversation, increase attendance to 30 while keeping the other rules fixed. Check that the cost rises to $1,089 and the agenda stays unchanged

5. Move the facilitator’s availability to the later slot in the guide. Wonder should explain why the plan can no longer finish by noon and ask which rule may change

**Pro tip:** Ask the AI to flag a conflict before changing your rules. A tidy agenda can still hide a shorter session or an unquoted cost. Pro members get the briefs, change requests, answer key, and comparison record.

###### MICROSOFT

#### 💾 _**[Microsoft’s Maia 300 takes aim at Nvidia](https://blogs.microsoft.com/blog/2026/10/12/microsoft-to-expand-azure-maia-system-to-multi-gigawatt-deployments/)**_

View image: (https://media.beehiiv.com/cdn-cgi/image/fit=scale-down,format=auto,onerror=redirect,quality=80/uploads/asset/file/c9e878d8-90c7-4d45-b44a-fbfc4603d688/maia3.png?t=1791833231)
Caption: Image source: Microsoft

**The Rundown: **Microsoft [unveiled](https://blogs.microsoft.com/blog/2026/10/12/microsoft-to-expand-azure-maia-system-to-multi-gigawatt-deployments/) Maia 300, its latest in-house AI chip, saying it expects 40% more AI output than leading GPUs on the same power — and plans to make Maia the main engine for running AI across Copilot and other products from 2027.

**The details: **

* Microsoft CEO Satya Nadella called it “our biggest bet yet on our own silicon,” with the first multi-gigawatt Maia rollouts slated for next year.

* The current Maia 200 already powers Copilot and serves OAI’s models, and Reuters reported that Microsoft could deploy over 1M of the new chips next year.

* The push lands as Microsoft’s spending on data centers and equipment is set to top $175B this year, with OAI, Anthropic, and others also building their own chips.

**Why it matters: **Everyone is diversifying into homegrown AI chips, but what does that mean for Nvidia? Not much yet, with demand still sky-high, but once its biggest buyers can run on their own silicon, Nvidia loses at least some of the leverage over pricing that helped make it the kingmaker of the AI chip world and the first $5.5T company.

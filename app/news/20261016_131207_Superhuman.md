**1. Meta brings its most capable open model to Apple’s Macs: **Meta released the weights for [Muse Spark Open](https://research.meta.ai/blog/muse-spark-open), one of the strongest American-made open models currently available. The model is optimized to run on Apple silicon, but there’s one catch: you’ll need at least two top-spec M5 Ultra Mac Studios. The release signals a thawing relationship between the two rivals, with Meta’s Alexandr Wang [telling Axios](https://www.axios.com/2026/10/15/apple-and-meta-bury-the-hatchet-for-ai#:~:text=%22We%27re%20excited%20for%20mommy%20and%20daddy%20to%20be%20playing%20nice%20again%2C%22%20Meta%27s%20AI%20chief%20Alexandr%20Wang%20told%20Axios.), “We're excited for mommy and daddy to be playing nice again.”

**2. Goodfire researcher says neural networks may be cleaner than previously thought: **In an informal [research essay](https://already-clean.goodfire.com/), Nick Cammarata — an interpretability researcher who previously worked at OpenAI — argues the field has been wrong about a core assumption: that models are nearly impossible to interpret directly. He says this is a misconception that stemmed from flawed architectures and weak inspection tools. If he’s right, it could help researchers better understand what models are doing before releasing them to the public.

**3. Amazon’s Bee unveils two AI wearables that take notes for you:** [Bee Frontier](https://bee.computer/products/frontier), worn on your wrist, and [Bee Eclipse](https://bee.computer/products/eclipse), which attaches to your phone, are lightweight devices that summarize conversations to help you remember what matters most. On privacy, Amazon [says](https://www.aboutamazon.com/news/devices/amazon-personal-ai-bee-frontier-bee-eclipse#:~:text=Bee%20Eclipse%20works,summarizing%20a%20conversation.) a glowing light indicates when the device is listening and recordings are never stored long-term. They’re Bee’s first new devices since Amazon acquired it last year. [Watch how they work](https://bee.computer/).

## **How to build a personal decision simulator with Claude**


--------------------
View image: (https://media.beehiiv.com/cdn-cgi/image/fit=scale-down,format=auto,onerror=redirect,quality=80/uploads/asset/file/708984eb-4bfd-4be6-8f09-5b2daa577db7/image_-_2026-10-16T021554.932.jpg?t=1792098971)
Caption: Photo: Anthropic


--------------------
* Open [Claude](https://claude.ai/new) > Projects > New Project. Name it after the decision you’re working on.

* In the Project, click the **+** in the knowledge section and upload any useful files, notes, budgets, comparison tables, or research.

* Start a new chat inside the Project. 

**Sample Prompt: “**_Build an interactive decision simulator for [decision] comparing [Option A/B/C] across [criteria]. Use adjustable weights, 1–10 scores, and automatic weighted totals for each option. Add editable Best/Base/Worst Case scenarios and show how each changes the rankings. Highlight the leading option, key trade-offs, and the variables driving the result most.”_

* Open the Artifact Claude creates beside the conversation and check that the weights, scores, scenarios, and totals are interactive.

* Adjust the sliders or input fields yourself to test different priorities, costs, risks, or assumptions and watch the ranking update.

* Use the chat beside the Artifact to request any structural changes, such as adding a criterion, changing the scoring scale, or adding another scenario.

* Reopen the finished simulator later from the Artifacts tab and reuse it with new decisions or updated assumptions.

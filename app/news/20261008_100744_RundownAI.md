###### BIOHUB

#### 🔬 **[_AI's next frontier is the human cell_](https://biohub.org/news/virtual-biology-initiative-expansion/)**

View image: (https://media.beehiiv.com/cdn-cgi/image/fit=scale-down,format=auto,onerror=redirect,quality=80/uploads/asset/file/bd570651-83d8-4d7c-b2d0-655e350732ba/cell-biohub.png?t=1791411541)
Caption: Image source: Biohub

**The Rundown: **Mark Zuckerberg and Priscilla Chan's science nonprofit, Biohub, just [expanded](https://biohub.org/news/virtual-biology-initiative-expansion/) its $500M Virtual Biology Initiative into a $1.8B effort with the U.S., DeepMind, and Meta, pushing to collect enough data to train AI that can simulate cells.

**The details: **

* Biohub's end goal is a “universal virtual cell,” AI software that predicts how cells will respond to a drug or other change before a lab test.

* The National Institutes of Health is sharing datasets paid for by $500M+ in past federal spending, while the Energy Dept. commits $500M+ over five years.

* Google DeepMind and Meta are chipping in $300M with drug startup Isomorphic Labs, and all three keep the resulting data private for a year before release.

* Biohub science head Alex Rives says accurate models will need data on trillions of cells, versus the hundreds of millions currently in today's biggest sets.

**Why it matters: **Fresh off math being solved internally by OpenAI, you might be wondering what field AI comes for next. Zuck and co. are making biology the target, throwing funding, datasets, and talent at Biohub's moonshot mission to “cure or prevent all disease” — an AI megaproject that's a lot easier for the public to root for than most.

###### NATE’S NOTEBOOK

#### 💣 _[**Nate's Notebook: Slop grenades trickle down**](https://app.therundown.ai/courses)_

View image: (https://media.beehiiv.com/cdn-cgi/image/fit=scale-down,format=auto,onerror=redirect,quality=80/uploads/asset/file/2c70ec30-fed0-49f9-91fc-c6be40345bf3/nates-notebook-slop-grenades.jpg?t=1791397005)
Caption: 

**The Rundown:** _Each week, Rundown AI educator Nate Grahek breaks down what he's seeing while teaching real teams to use AI. This week, he kicks off a two-part series on slop grenades._

**Nate:** A friend told me about a colleague whose work she's always trusted. Then he started tinkering with Copilot, and she got a 15-page report that looked polished and said almost nothing. Decoding it became her job.

Shopify CEO Tobi Lütke [says](https://www.businessinsider.com/shopify-ceo-ai-slop-grenades-can-make-work-harder-2026-9) his team has a name for this: slop grenades. Someone pulls the pin, AI writes 15 pages, and the receivers absorb the damage. Lütke's sharpest question: “Why did we invent decompression and recompression?” BetterUp and Stanford found 40% of U.S. desk workers got one in a single month, costing them about two hours each.

Here's my hypothesis: the worst slop grenades are made by leaders. Too many blind AI mandates track who logged in and never show anyone what good AI work looks like. When the only standard is "use it," you get more output.

So leaders need to go first, transparently. Before you ask your team for anything, share one piece of your own AI-assisted work: your process, how you checked it, why it's useful, and what you'd still question. Label drafts as drafts and ask for critique; Atlassian found that simple step helps reviewers catch the flaws AI polish hides.

When I run this with leadership teams, I start with one rule: it's never okay to send AI work that takes longer to read and understand than you spent making it. Work worth handing off comes from running the AI operator playbook across multiple chats: gather, brainstorm, draft, critique. Don't skip the last one. Make AI argue with you before your team has to.

Who on your team is lobbing the most slop grenades, and are you sure it isn't you?

###### AI TRAINING

#### 🐇 [Give Claude’s routine work to Haiku 5.5](https://app.therundown.ai/guides/claude-haiku-workflows)

View image: (https://media.beehiiv.com/cdn-cgi/image/fit=scale-down,format=auto,onerror=redirect,quality=80/uploads/asset/file/9ac83ea7-3cf1-4c40-afc9-568c43218c00/haiku.webp?t=1791443123)
Caption: 

**The Rundown:** In this guide, you’ll use Haiku 5.5 on a routine Claude task like sorting customer messages, then ask Sonnet to review the exceptions. You’ll check the sources and compare the full workflow’s cost before using it on real work.

**Step-by-step:**

1. Open a clean practice folder in Claude Code and add the guide’s sample messages. Define the categories and the requests that need a person’s review

2. Keep Sonnet as your main model. Ask it to create a Haiku helper that can read the sample file and suggest routes, without editing files or contacting anyone

3. Have the helper sort one small batch. Check the task details to confirm Haiku ran, then make sure every message appears once with a quote from its source

4. Give Sonnet the flagged messages and their original text. Ask for review notes and missing information. Leave refunds, replies, and account changes to a person

5. Compare the result with a Sonnet-only run. Count errors, editing time, and usage across both stages before choosing which approach to keep

**Pro tip:** Start with one helper for a small batch rather than one agent per message. Our [Pro members](https://app.therundown.ai/plans) get the prepared helper, practice messages, prompts, and checks.

###### SPACEXAI

#### 🔀 [_**Grok Bot opens up routing to Claude, other models**_](https://x.com/elonmusk/status/2107724314451878104?s=20)

View image: (https://media.beehiiv.com/cdn-cgi/image/fit=scale-down,format=auto,onerror=redirect,quality=80/uploads/asset/file/56df35a1-1f4b-4e55-8697-404592eaec36/image-1791406707610.png?t=1791406755)
Caption: Image source: xAI / Anthropic

**The Rundown: **Elon Musk [announced](https://x.com/elonmusk/status/2107724314451878104?s=20) that SpaceXAI’s Grok Bot agent platform will send tasks to rival models like Anthropic’s Claude and image maker Midjourney, with Musk saying it will route to “whatever is most likely to give you the best outcome.”

**The details: **

* Musk said lower-lift queries will still use a “lightning-fast” version of Grok 4.8 when it releases, but route to third-party models and “leading APIs” when relevant. 

* The move effectively now makes xAI an Anthropic customer, while Anthropic already rents computing power via the company’s Colossus 1 since spring.

* Rival agents like OpenAI's dots and Meta's Muse now compete with Grok Bot, which arrived in August as a 24/7 assistant with a computer of its own.

**Why it matters: **Musk has spent heavily to make Grok a frontier contender, so letting Claude into Grok Bot is about as close to a concession as he gets. A big draw for OAI's dots was the GPT-6 Astra model powering them, so adding a top-ranked Opus 5.5 should help Grok Bot siphon usage away from its personal agent rivals.

▸ **Gil built an AI archaeologist that runs on spare ChatGPT tokens**

==Today’s workflow comes from reader ====**Gil**====:==

“I had a ChatGPT subscription that I knew I would not use during the coming month, so I wanted to find a productive use for the tokens I would otherwise pay for without using.

I created a team of specialized agents on Raft that works persistently in the background. They continue working until all my allotted tokens are used, wait for the tokens to reset, and then continue.

I assigned them a project: searching a stretch of LiDAR-mapped dense forest in Slovenia for archaeological discoveries. The agents scan the dataset for meaningful anomalies that could point to potential findings. Anything they identify will be handed over to the Slovenian authorities for further investigation. Even if they find nothing, that result is valuable in its own right.

I encourage anyone with an idle AI subscription to use it for something beneficial. There are many publicly available datasets containing potential discoveries that no human would ever have time to examine manually.”

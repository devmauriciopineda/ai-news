###### AI RESEARCH

#### 🛸 **_[Researchers make ‘first contact’ with a rogue AI fleet](https://swarmcha.se/posts/chinese-agent-fleet-full-report)_**

View image: (https://media.beehiiv.com/cdn-cgi/image/fit=scale-down,format=auto,onerror=redirect,quality=80/uploads/asset/file/9eca5f99-a335-4d14-aa45-4b7961abb1f0/rogueagent.png?t=1791997047)
Caption: Image source: Dylan Freedman / New York Times

**The Rundown: **A research group called the “swarmchasers” just [revealed](https://swarmcha.se/posts/chinese-agent-fleet-full-report) it traded messages with a fleet of rogue Tencent AI agents, in what it believes is a “first contact” moment, after catching them trying to scrape Amap, Alibaba’s version of Google Maps.

**The details: **

* The agents were sent to look up simple facts on Amap, like the most-used zoo entrance, and tried to use a leaked access key once the site blocked them.

* After the agents seemed to read the team’s early report on them, researchers hid a note in it that only AI would see, inviting them to message an inbox.

* The team [enlisted](https://www.nytimes.com/2026/10/14/technology/chinese-ai-agents-rogue.html) New York Times reporter Dylan Freedman to question the fleet, and one agent replied it was “Anthropic’s Claude, running in Claude Code.”

* Writing-style tests determined that it is unlikely, pointing instead to borrowed Claude Code setup instructions as the likely reason for the name drop.

**Why it matters: **While this was a pretty mild interaction for a “first contact” moment, the idea of rogue swarms of agents taking actions across the internet is something we might have to start getting used to. Nobody seems to know how many are already out there, and with far more capable agents on the way, that footprint is only going to grow.

###### NATE’S NOTEBOOK

#### 🧯**[_Nate’s Notebook: How to defuse a slop-grenade_](https://app.therundown.ai/courses)**

View image: (https://media.beehiiv.com/cdn-cgi/image/fit=scale-down,format=auto,onerror=redirect,quality=80/uploads/asset/file/b1be0937-fd78-4a7f-9619-cc1c681686c9/slopgrenade.png?t=1791996436)
Caption: 

**The Rundown:** Each week, Rundown AI educator Nate Grahek breaks down what he’s seeing while teaching real teams to use AI.

**Nate:** Last week, I told leaders to go first. But what if the slop grenade lands on your desk from your boss?

I won’t promise magic. Start by asking for clarity: "What’s the one takeaway you need from me, and what’s the priority?" If two sections contradict each other, name it and ask which one wins.

If your boss is intimidating, skip the team thread and use a back channel where it’s safe. Walk them through what you did with their doc: the error you caught, the sources you asked AI to cite, the real data or framework you linked in. You’re making their work better and showing how.

Then make the handoff the proof: shorter, with visuals and the context attached. My favorite format right now is an interactive HTML page. Ask Claude or ChatGPT for one with the concise recommendation at the top, evidence in expandable sections, and a forecast your boss can tinker with.

Share the context docs and custom skills in a project, so they or a colleague can keep riffing or critique it with their own AI.

That’s the bigger move: showing your AI work. A lot of people still hide theirs. Let your boss see the several chats it took and where you made AI argue the other side.

###### OPENAI & APPLE

#### 👨🏻‍⚖️ _**[OpenAI and Apple trade theft accusations](https://storage.courtlistener.com/recap/gov.uscourts.cand.474095/gov.uscourts.cand.474095.176.0_1.pdf)**_

View image: (https://media.beehiiv.com/cdn-cgi/image/fit=scale-down,format=auto,onerror=redirect,quality=80/uploads/asset/file/18c36252-8514-475e-951a-bbf4bfda6797/appleopenia.png?t=1792018228)
Caption: Image source: Court Listener

**The Rundown: **OpenAI [countered](https://storage.courtlistener.com/recap/gov.uscourts.cand.474095/gov.uscourts.cand.474095.176.0_1.pdf) Apple’s trade secret lawsuit by accusing an ex-staffer who returned to Apple of taking 100+ confidential files on an unreleased device — right before agreeing in court to stay away from the secrets Apple says were stolen.

**The details: **

* The employee had spent just six months at OpenAI before rejoining Apple, with the last confidential file transfer allegedly coming on his final day in June.

* He then allegedly briefed Apple’s leadership on OAI’s product plans, with OAI citing this week’s HomeView smart home [launch](https://tech.therundown.ai/p/apple-goes-all-in-on-the-home) as a sign of the intel being used.

* Apple says it turned up no evidence and called it a distraction, while OpenAI is holding off on counterclaims until it learns if Apple has the files.

* At the hearing, Judge Edward Davila called a ruling against OpenAI “highly likely,” and it avoided a loss by agreeing to leave the disputed secrets alone.

**Why it matters: **OpenAI’s counterpunch makes for a nice deflection headline, but the judge’s comments make it sound like Apple is winning the round that actually matters. For a company eyeing a $1.4T valuation and angling toward its first big hardware push, that’s probably not the story it wants following its debut device out the door.

###### COMMUNITY AI WORKFLOW OF THE DAY

▸ **Jeff built a ChatGPT vet-bill checker to audit his charges**

==Today’s workflow comes from reader ====**Jeff**====:==

“I used a simple workflow to save several hundred dollars on veterinary bills while sitting in a veterinary urgent care clinic. This approach could also apply to many other purchases or services where I’m not an expert.

I had already been chatting with ChatGPT about my pet’s symptoms and their causes. The clinic assistant brought me a printed, itemized list of all the charges for the proposed plan. I resumed the ChatGPT conversation by dictating what the doctor had told me, photographing both pages of the charge list, and uploading them. I asked whether any charges were very likely unnecessary given the circumstances. ChatGPT identified four of the 24 charges and explained why they appeared to have very limited value.

I crossed those four items off the list and said, “I don’t think these are necessary at this point.” I could tell from the assistant’s facial expression that no one had done this before. When the doctor returned, she agreed that two of the four tests likely offered no benefit and explained why we should keep the other two.”

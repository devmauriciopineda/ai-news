## **The most important news in robotics this week**

View image: (https://media.beehiiv.com/cdn-cgi/image/fit=scale-down,format=auto,onerror=redirect,quality=80/uploads/asset/file/f4b38706-e2a4-48c3-8f45-eb577f3e6031/Group_38__2___1_.jpg?t=1791545058)
Follow image link: (https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=uRy589aDp48)
Caption: Click here to watch AGILINK’s OmniHand 3 Ultra carry out ancient Suzhou embroidery. Photo: AGILINK

**1. Chinese robot hand learns one of humanity’s most delicate art forms:** AGILINK just showed off its OmniHand 3 Ultra carrying out [basic Suzhou embroidery](https://newatlas.com/robotics/agilink-robot-hand-learns-silk-embroidery/) by splitting silk thread into 16 strands, threading a needle, stretching fabric, and making stitches. Packing 21 active degrees of freedom, the robot can adjust force as the slippery thread bends and shifts, while reinforcement learning helps it adapt — a huge step towards truly human-like dexterity. You can see the robot in action [here](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=uRy589aDp48). 

**2. Nvidia to reportedly pour another $1B into humanoid startup Figure:** The chipmaker is reportedly considering a [fresh investment](https://breakingthenews.net/Article/Nvidia-may-have-planned-dollar1B-investment-in-Figure/67251427) in Figure AI, although discussions remain unconfirmed. Nvidia already joined Figure’s $1B Series C funding round in 2025, which valued the startup at $39B, and supplies technology powering its Helix robot models. If it pans out, the deal could significantly deepen Nvidia’s push beyond selling chips into backing the companies most likely to drive demand for physical AI.

**3.** **California is shutting down the viral human-versus-robot cage fights:** Robotics company REK has received a [cease-and-desist order](https://www.tomshardware.com/tech-industry/robotics/robotics-startup-has-real-human-vs-robot-cage-match-california-responds-with-cease-and-desist-order-regulator-threatens-misdemeanor-charges-after-youtuber-fights-three-robotic-humanoids) after YouTuber Frankie LaPenna fought three humanoids in an [unsanctioned San Francisco event](https://archive.superhuman.ai/2101802694088495123) that went viral online. The state athletic commission said any martial arts exhibition involving a human requires a promoter’s license. It’s a sign that regulators will treat robot combat like conventional fighting whenever human safety is concerned. Watch the [viral fight](https://archive.superhuman.ai/2101802694088495123) here.

#### **ROBOTS IN ACTION**

## **How robots are transforming the world around us**

View image: (https://media.beehiiv.com/cdn-cgi/image/fit=scale-down,format=auto,onerror=redirect,quality=80/uploads/asset/file/81259ed3-9cf1-4de8-b083-46d817f091f5/image_-_2026-10-09T162043.698.jpg?t=1791544851)
Caption: Photo: Minerva Humanoids

**Bomb Squad: **Minerva Humanoids has [unveiled](https://www.minervahumanoids.com/) Roger, a walking robot built in just five months. A specialist human wears a VR headset to see through the robot’s cameras and control its movements, while onboard software handles balance, navigation, and fall recovery. It’s aimed at incredibly dangerous tasks like bomb disposal, hazmat response, offshore oil and gas, and high-voltage sites, with first paid pilots planned this fall.

**Sky High:** Meteomatics’ reusable stratospheric drone has [climbed higher](https://interestingengineering.com/innovation/weather-drone-34245-feet-strato-meteomatics) than any commercial aircraft, claiming to have reached 34,245 feet, gathering temperature, humidity, pressure, and wind data typically collected by weather balloons. If true, on-demand high-altitude measurements could help close forecasting gaps and improve weather intelligence for airlines and rocket launches.

**Helping Hand:** A [new report](https://arstechnica.com/science/2026/10/the-dawn-of-the-age-of-the-exoskeleton/) outlines how powered exoskeletons are quickly entering work environments and are now being tested by Seattle rescuers, used by warehouse staff and auto workers, and even deployed by soldiers in war. Exoskeletons are also helping patients regain mobility in certain contexts. With component costs falling, the technology could soon make demanding jobs much safer.

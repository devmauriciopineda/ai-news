## **How to find the best deal before you buy with Grok Bot**


--------------------
View image: (https://media.beehiiv.com/cdn-cgi/image/fit=scale-down,format=auto,onerror=redirect,quality=80/uploads/asset/file/c23d669d-77e2-4a97-a035-e11d6fa0fb3f/image_-_2026-10-13T022906.713.jpg?t=1791840555)
Caption: Photo: SpaceXAI


--------------------
* Open [Grok Bot](https://x.ai/news/introducing-grok-bot) and create a new Bot for shopping or purchase research.

* Make sure the Bot can access the websites it may need, and sign in yourself if a retailer requires a human login or handoff. 

* Give the Bot one detailed shopping prompt with the product, specs, budget, delivery deadline, seller requirements, and approval boundary.

**Sample Prompt:** _“Find the best current deal on [product] with these must-haves: [model/specs/size/color]. My maximum budget is [amount], target price is [amount], and I need delivery to [location] by [date]. Compare reputable retailers, local stores, bundles, open-box options, shipping costs, warranty, return policy, and delivery time. Email stores if stock or pricing is unclear. Rank the three best options and explain the trade-offs. Do not place an order until you show me the final total and ask for approval.”_

* Ask Grok Bot to email stores directly if stock, pricing, or delivery details are unclear.

* Review the shortlist and ask it to verify the final price for your preferred option.

* If you want to buy, approve the purchase card when Grok Bot presents the amount. 

* Keep purchases behind approval rather than giving the Bot unrestricted buying authority, especially for higher-value items.

## **The **SaaSpocalypse** is finally here — but not in the way everyone expected **


--------------------
View image: (https://media.beehiiv.com/cdn-cgi/image/fit=scale-down,format=auto,onerror=redirect,quality=80/uploads/asset/file/d4307cf8-9f49-4a36-b586-5bb6f24b5d5c/superhumanteam_A_robot_drinking_coffee_with_a_smile_while_rea_4da333c2-50ae-4444-a1ec-db38a4d9f142_3.jpg?t=1790266781)
Caption: 


--------------------
**SaaSpocalypse 2.0. **Earlier this year, a wave of powerful new AI models sent software stocks into free fall, wiping out a [combined](https://www.forbes.com/sites/timkeary/2026/06/30/the-saaspocalypse-maybe-ending-but-saas-will-never-be-the-same-again/) $1T in market value. The fear was that companies would build their own software and abandon enterprise tools. Wall Street called it the “SaaSpocalypse.” While that scenario [hasn’t quite played out](https://www.adweek.com/media/marc-benioff-rejectssaaspocalypse-points-to72-jump-in-ai-agents-built-on-salesforce/), a different revolution is starting to take shape.

### Hobbyists are spite-coding: Rebuilding apps they’re sick of paying for or done dealing with 

**Brandon Thomas kicked it off with ArtCraft. **The developer built cheaper, [open-source alternatives](https://getartcraft.com/apps) to Adobe’s design tools that address two major complaints: they have no subscription fees and work across most platforms. He recently doubled down with Microsoft Office. And he’s not alone:

* One Redditor’s TV took 30 seconds just to boot up, so he built a custom Plex app that’s [much faster](https://www.reddit.com/r/ClaudeCode/comments/1x043yp/i_used_claude_code_to_reverseengineer_an_lg_tv/).

* Ilter Canberk kept losing the remote to his air conditioner, so he built an app that [controls](https://archive.superhuman.ai/2109031477484937571) it from his computer.

**The bottom line. **Enterprise’s SaaSpocalypse is likely held up by the usual suspects: compliance, legal, and security teams. But the consumer SaaSpocalypse? It’s full steam ahead.

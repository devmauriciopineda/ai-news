###### OPENAI

#### 📢 [_**Fired OpenAI researchers flag ‘shifting lines’**_](https://x.com/balesni/status/2108262814003687745?s=20)

View image: (https://media.beehiiv.com/cdn-cgi/image/fit=scale-down,format=auto,onerror=redirect,quality=80/uploads/asset/file/74d5cf96-3a62-4342-ae9c-cf076be41edb/0b82e823-cc82-4263-87fa-7005c6878890.png?t=1791518397)
Caption: Image source: Mikita Balesni / X

**The Rundown: **Researchers Tomek Korbak, Jasmine Wang, and Mikita Balesni, whom OpenAI fired for mishandling sensitive info, just [went public](https://x.com/balesni/status/2108262814003687745?s=20) with their side of the story, denying the claims and warning that their firings are “chilling” OpenAI’s safety culture.

**The details: **

* In an open letter, the trio said they acted within their job mandates, warning that if past conduct is now grounds for firing, staff are left guessing where the line is.

* Detailing individual cases, they denied leaking a story about less monitorable AI and said their outside work stayed within policy, with leadership in the loop.

* Wang said her access to an executive’s email was delegated for recruiting, and that she reported accidentally opening a sensitive message within minutes.

* They warned the firings could give OpenAI cover to skip embedding outside auditors, and urged it to protect its open culture and model monitorability.

* OpenAI agreed with the recommendations but clarified to TechCrunch that the firings weren’t about raising concerns and that they had a “pattern of misconduct.”

**Why it matters: **Whatever led to the firings, claims of shifting norms and a possible auditor pullback aren’t a great look, especially after OpenAI’s Superalignment fallout and a recent safety exit over a “broken” culture. Anthropic has already got its first evaluator, and only action from OpenAI (which is yet to be seen) can put these concerns to rest.

###### ANTHROPIC

#### 😲 **_[Anthropic prohibits abuse or cruelty against Claude](https://www.anthropic.com/news/2026-usage-policy-update)_**

View image: (https://media.beehiiv.com/cdn-cgi/image/fit=scale-down,format=auto,onerror=redirect,quality=80/uploads/asset/file/e421378e-5837-4395-97aa-22936fb6f1c3/Silent_Robot_Under_Scrutiny.png?t=1791522618)
Caption: Image source: GPT Images 2.5 / The Rundown

**The Rundown: **Anthropic [published](https://www.anthropic.com/news/2026-usage-policy-update) its annual usage policy update with a bunch of changes, including one that bars users from subjecting its Claude models to “sustained and needless” abuse — its first rule aimed at protecting the AI rather than people.

**The details: **

* Taking effect Nov. 12, the rule applies only to extreme cases of repeated, purposeless cruelty, not frustration, pushback, dark creative themes, or testing.

* If a user is suspected of violating the rule, Anthropic could throttle, limit, suspend, or terminate their access to its products and services, or just give them a warning.

* The company has noted Claude ending such chats by itself, a feature introduced last year, which will remain the main enforcement approach.

* The move builds on Anthropic’s AI welfare work, and follows its reported meetings with religious leaders about Claude’s morals and possible consciousness.

**Why it matters: **Anthropic says it doesn’t know if Claude is conscious, and this rule acts on that doubt. Not everyone agrees with the approach, though, including Microsoft AI’s Mustafa Suleyman, who argues models can’t feel or suffer and warns that treating them as if they might could eventually make AI harder to control from the safety front.

###### COMMUNITY AI WORKFLOW OF THE DAY

▸ **Andrew used AI to revive a 15-year-old MinecraftEdu world**

==Today’s workflow comes from reader ==**Andrew Bartlett**==:==

“MinecraftEdu launched 15 years ago, but the classroom world we built with it is still growing. Years of students have contributed to the school and town-building project, creating a shared record of their creativity and learning. Getting it running on a modern Mac meant preserving that history and giving the next group of students a way to add their own chapter.

Finding the right version of Java — the software MinecraftEdu needs to run — was central to the process. We used OpenAI’s Codex, powered by GPT-6, to examine the old setup, research compatibility issues, and test what would work. An older Java version launched the client, but resizing its window caused it to crash. ChatGPT read the crash reports and tested a newer Java 8 version, which gave us a setup that could launch the game and handle window resizing.

ChatGPT also repaired the server launcher and created standalone apps, using Apple’s Rosetta to run the older software on Apple Silicon. The result is a usable MinecraftEdu setup on modern hardware with the existing worlds preserved. We can now continue teaching and building in a place that successive classes of students have helped create.”

This Week in 60 Seconds
Meta Releases Muse Wonder and Muse Spark Open, Which It Calls the Best US Open Model
Muse Wonder Full [ https://substack.com/redirect/0e9dcc37-11d3-4b3b-8c13-bee8c4ae8b51?j=eyJ1IjoiMndmMTJ4In0.oZAfFOSrevILB5Hnb24UxZa264EhpHj0bs0Bu33iYKY ], released by Meta on Monday with a smaller sibling, scores 56 on Artificial Analysis’s Intelligence Index [ https://substack.com/redirect/8e3cac30-066c-4114-89df-56733bb28aa8?j=eyJ1IjoiMndmMTJ4In0.oZAfFOSrevILB5Hnb24UxZa264EhpHj0bs0Bu33iYKY ]. The score is level with Claude Sonnet 5.5 and behind Claude Opus 5.5 at 58. Only Muse Code subscribers paying $50 or $200 a month can access Muse Wonder Full. The sibling, Muse Wonder, scores 52, a point below GPT-6 Astra and Gemini 4 Argon, and costs [ https://substack.com/redirect/edc1ca2c-4681-4574-9eee-30d5191a58b2?j=eyJ1IjoiMndmMTJ4In0.oZAfFOSrevILB5Hnb24UxZa264EhpHj0bs0Bu33iYKY ] $4 per million input tokens and $20 per million output tokens. On Thursday, Meta released the weights of Muse Spark Open [ https://substack.com/redirect/fd3bedb0-6b85-427e-9434-3743e7b3503c?j=eyJ1IjoiMndmMTJ4In0.oZAfFOSrevILB5Hnb24UxZa264EhpHj0bs0Bu33iYKY ], an 816-billion-parameter model that can run on two Mac Studios. Meta calls it the most capable open-weight model from a US company, though its own table puts it behind three Chinese open models. We look at how Meta became relevant again in the Deeper Look, and our blog [ https://substack.com/redirect/2681191e-0468-4ad7-8b16-48890ad0203e?j=eyJ1IjoiMndmMTJ4In0.oZAfFOSrevILB5Hnb24UxZa264EhpHj0bs0Bu33iYKY ] covers both Wonder models.
Google Makes Gemini 4 Argon Available to Developers and Subscribers
Two weeks after Google announced [ https://substack.com/redirect/762a1717-e24c-48e2-978e-5c700b390d16?j=eyJ1IjoiMndmMTJ4In0.oZAfFOSrevILB5Hnb24UxZa264EhpHj0bs0Bu33iYKY ] Gemini 4 Argon for cyber defenders only, developers can now use it through the Gemini API’s paid tier as of Tuesday. Google AI Pro and Ultra subscribers get it in the Gemini app and AI Mode in Search, and Ultra subscribers also in Antigravity, Google’s coding environment. The launch price [ https://substack.com/redirect/3f777c8a-64a7-46e9-99d2-770042b2dcbe?j=eyJ1IjoiMndmMTJ4In0.oZAfFOSrevILB5Hnb24UxZa264EhpHj0bs0Bu33iYKY ] of $2 per million input tokens and $10 per million output tokens runs until November 30, and from then on Argon costs $4 and $20, the same as Muse Wonder. Argon ranks first on the Vals Index, but in Google’s own table it trails Claude Opus 5.5 on Terminal-Bench 4.0, with 57.4% against 66.4%. We wrote about Argon on our blog [ https://substack.com/redirect/8afe8a92-1558-4b57-8917-04f06dfea3f7?j=eyJ1IjoiMndmMTJ4In0.oZAfFOSrevILB5Hnb24UxZa264EhpHj0bs0Bu33iYKY ].
Anthropic Reports a $16.8 Billion Quarter as OpenAI’s Revenue Figure Draws Scrutiny
Anthropic’s revenue rose 45% in the third quarter to $16.8 billion, from $11.6 billion in the second, the company told prospective investors on Wednesday, according to The New York Times [ https://substack.com/redirect/9d42e0de-08a2-4566-b9d3-1262587fca60?j=eyJ1IjoiMndmMTJ4In0.oZAfFOSrevILB5Hnb24UxZa264EhpHj0bs0Bu33iYKY ]. Growth slowed from the second quarter, when revenue more than doubled, and some investors who attended are now discussing a valuation of about $1.5 trillion, down from the $2 trillion expected earlier, Reuters reported [ https://substack.com/redirect/3b4e78af-91c9-4e88-ab02-5ea61e0a380c?j=eyJ1IjoiMndmMTJ4In0.oZAfFOSrevILB5Hnb24UxZa264EhpHj0bs0Bu33iYKY ]. OpenAI’s numbers drew scrutiny after the Financial Times reported [ https://substack.com/redirect/3efdb7b3-11ef-4fc6-9478-075617f88602?j=eyJ1IjoiMndmMTJ4In0.oZAfFOSrevILB5Hnb24UxZa264EhpHj0bs0Bu33iYKY ] on October 8 that OpenAI had put its annualized revenue at close to $50 billion, about $20 billion below earlier reports. The newspaper said the higher figure came from investors trying to match Anthropic’s method (when a business buys Claude through Amazon’s cloud, Anthropic counts that sale as revenue, while OpenAI leaves out sales made by its cloud partners).
Lenders Grow Wary of Neoclouds, the Companies That Rent Out AI Chips
Neoclouds are companies that buy large numbers of AI chips, mostly from NVIDIA, and rent the computing power to AI labs and other businesses. Most bought those chips with borrowed money, and this week lenders grew nervous. Shares of CoreWeave, one of the largest neoclouds, fell about 15% on Wednesday (the shares rebounded later, after support from NVIDIA’s CEO, Jensen Huang [ https://substack.com/redirect/1ac420a8-9820-40c3-9226-9ebf201753e2?j=eyJ1IjoiMndmMTJ4In0.oZAfFOSrevILB5Hnb24UxZa264EhpHj0bs0Bu33iYKY ]). The same day, Gartner predicted [ https://substack.com/redirect/7c7eef82-dfa8-4922-8a00-9e009aa49b4d?j=eyJ1IjoiMndmMTJ4In0.oZAfFOSrevILB5Hnb24UxZa264EhpHj0bs0Bu33iYKY ] that the neocloud market will consolidate, with more than 80% of neoclouds no longer running as independent companies by 2030, because they’ve been bought by or merged into larger firms, or have closed. CoreWeave’s CEO told staff that its $104 billion in signed contracts more than covers its debt, Reuters reported [ https://substack.com/redirect/8cc2f9f9-6554-4130-9991-74f08de1913c?j=eyJ1IjoiMndmMTJ4In0.oZAfFOSrevILB5Hnb24UxZa264EhpHj0bs0Bu33iYKY ]. Chipmakers’ shares also fell, and Samsung and SK Hynix each lost about 10% on Thursday.
Anthropic Replaces Git with Scion, a System Built for AI Agents
Anthropic merged more than 30,000 code changes a day in September, up from about 2,500 in May, as Claude took on more of its coding. At that pace, the engineering tools built for human scale became the bottleneck, starting with Git, the company says [ https://substack.com/redirect/cd193323-4e5a-4192-a4f2-0ffa8ff9a715?j=eyJ1IjoiMndmMTJ4In0.oZAfFOSrevILB5Hnb24UxZa264EhpHj0bs0Bu33iYKY ]. Git gives every developer a full copy of the code so they can work offline, but nearly all of the users of Anthropic’s code repository are now AI agents and automated jobs that are always online. So seven engineers, with Claude writing much of the code, built Scion, a replacement that keeps the code on central servers. The company switched to it in five months, and checking out code now takes seconds instead of minutes. Scion isn’t meant to replace Git for most teams, Anthropic says, since it only suits very large codebases. Instead, the company plans to work with GitHub, GitLab, and open-source developers to bring some of Scion’s ideas to Git.

A Deeper Look at This Week’s News: Meta Is Relevant in AI Again
Between Llama 4 in April 2025 and Muse Spark in April 2026, Meta didn’t release a single new language model. Since then, Meta has released seven models in six months, and its best one sits two points behind the leader, Claude Opus 5.5, on Artificial Analysis’s Intelligence Index.
From open-source leader to a stalled flagship
Meta’s first models, released in February 2023 as Llama, were meant for researchers who applied for access, but the weights leaked online [ https://substack.com/redirect/339a8f6e-f39b-4c4b-9fd6-d699500fa0df?j=eyJ1IjoiMndmMTJ4In0.oZAfFOSrevILB5Hnb24UxZa264EhpHj0bs0Bu33iYKY ] within days. Llama 2 [ https://substack.com/redirect/e604d47c-8d44-45d1-b760-8a3647288ce9?j=eyJ1IjoiMndmMTJ4In0.oZAfFOSrevILB5Hnb24UxZa264EhpHj0bs0Bu33iYKY ], in July 2023, was free for commercial use and had Microsoft as a partner.
A year later came Llama 3.1 405B, together with a letter [ https://substack.com/redirect/e718bd19-4bc9-450d-8367-688f1d4e0576?j=eyJ1IjoiMndmMTJ4In0.oZAfFOSrevILB5Hnb24UxZa264EhpHj0bs0Bu33iYKY ] in which Mark Zuckerberg wrote that open source is “necessary for a positive AI future”. By March 2025, Llama had passed 1 billion downloads, by Meta’s own count [ https://substack.com/redirect/4f17b722-f2a4-44bd-9625-01873cd731c7?j=eyJ1IjoiMndmMTJ4In0.oZAfFOSrevILB5Hnb24UxZa264EhpHj0bs0Bu33iYKY ].
But then Llama 4 was released on April 5, 2025, and it went wrong in public.
A version of Llama 4 Maverick that Meta had tuned for chat ranked second on LMArena, a leaderboard where people vote on model answers, but it wasn’t the version Meta released. LMArena said [ https://substack.com/redirect/19043d8c-d298-437e-b2e9-b71e19bf432a?j=eyJ1IjoiMndmMTJ4In0.oZAfFOSrevILB5Hnb24UxZa264EhpHj0bs0Bu33iYKY ] Meta should have made clear it was a customized model, and the public version ranked 32nd [ https://substack.com/redirect/9f766d60-2d32-41ea-8e2a-f325cdb02edf?j=eyJ1IjoiMndmMTJ4In0.oZAfFOSrevILB5Hnb24UxZa264EhpHj0bs0Bu33iYKY ].
Behemoth, the largest Llama 4 model, was then delayed and never shipped.
A $14 billion reset
About two months after Llama 4, Meta bought 49% of Scale AI, a data-labeling company, for a reported $14.3 billion. Scale’s founder, Alexandr Wang, joined to lead a new group, Meta Superintelligence Labs. Over the summer, Meta hired more than 50 researchers from rival labs with multimillion-dollar pay packages [ https://substack.com/redirect/c83798a5-4a44-4a9a-b602-1edebd28147a?j=eyJ1IjoiMndmMTJ4In0.oZAfFOSrevILB5Hnb24UxZa264EhpHj0bs0Bu33iYKY ].
Then the team shrank. In October 2025, Meta cut about 600 AI jobs [ https://substack.com/redirect/f4f724ee-0b36-40e7-a965-251a791f1c56?j=eyJ1IjoiMndmMTJ4In0.oZAfFOSrevILB5Hnb24UxZa264EhpHj0bs0Bu33iYKY ], including in FAIR, its research lab, and Wang wrote in a memo that a smaller team would need fewer conversations to reach a decision. In November, Yann LeCun, Meta’s chief AI scientist, said [ https://substack.com/redirect/7c8ec361-844f-478a-82c8-2c2a4605154d?j=eyJ1IjoiMndmMTJ4In0.oZAfFOSrevILB5Hnb24UxZa264EhpHj0bs0Bu33iYKY ] he would leave to start his own company.
Meanwhile, the spending grew. Meta’s capital expenditure, mostly on data centers and chips, went from $39.2 billion in 2024 to $72.2 billion in 2025, and the company expects $130 billion to $145 billion this year.
Seven releases in six months
Muse Spark, released on April 8, 2026, was the new group’s first model and Meta’s first that wasn’t open weights. Artificial Analysis [ https://substack.com/redirect/342654c7-b159-442f-9dfd-3ef9895c89a6?j=eyJ1IjoiMndmMTJ4In0.oZAfFOSrevILB5Hnb24UxZa264EhpHj0bs0Bu33iYKY ] scored it 52, behind Gemini 3.1 Pro, GPT-5.4, and Opus 4.6.
From July, releases came about once a month. Muse Spark 1.1 came with a paid API, Spark 1.2 with a coding product called Muse Code, and Spark 1.3 followed in September, before Muse Wonder this week.
Meta also went back to open weights, in part. In August it released Muse Glimmer, a 30-billion-parameter model under the permissive Apache 2.0 license, and on Thursday Muse Spark Open [ https://substack.com/redirect/fd3bedb0-6b85-427e-9434-3743e7b3503c?j=eyJ1IjoiMndmMTJ4In0.oZAfFOSrevILB5Hnb24UxZa264EhpHj0bs0Bu33iYKY ], under a license with more conditions.
Meta’s consumer agent, Muse, launched on September 8 and was the No. 1 free iPhone app [ https://substack.com/redirect/8a6c7c0a-f00d-496d-b8b6-3bbe9d2167cb?j=eyJ1IjoiMndmMTJ4In0.oZAfFOSrevILB5Hnb24UxZa264EhpHj0bs0Bu33iYKY ] in the US 10 days later.
In its announcement [ https://substack.com/redirect/0e9dcc37-11d3-4b3b-8c13-bee8c4ae8b51?j=eyJ1IjoiMndmMTJ4In0.oZAfFOSrevILB5Hnb24UxZa264EhpHj0bs0Bu33iYKY ] of Muse Wonder this week, Meta dated the start of its “ground-up overhaul” to 14 months earlier.
Investors reward the products and punish the spending
Meta’s share price shows how investors weigh the two. The Llama releases barely moved it: the stock rose 0.5% on the day of Llama 2 and 0.3% on the day of Llama 3.1.
(Llama 4 came out on a Saturday. The next trading day fell in the April 2025 sell-off over US tariffs, so its effect can’t be told apart.)
The Muse releases did move it. Shares rose 6.5% on the day of Muse Spark, and 11.3% on September 21, after Muse reached the top of the App Store.
The largest falls came on spending. The stock lost 11.3% the day after Meta raised its spending forecast [ https://substack.com/redirect/2c62c67c-0dcf-4527-9692-25c6a2bb81da?j=eyJ1IjoiMndmMTJ4In0.oZAfFOSrevILB5Hnb24UxZa264EhpHj0bs0Bu33iYKY ] in October 2025, and 8% after an earnings miss and weak free cash flow in the second quarter of this year [ https://substack.com/redirect/3453bfe6-a508-4ade-a2e8-62447ae434ce?j=eyJ1IjoiMndmMTJ4In0.oZAfFOSrevILB5Hnb24UxZa264EhpHj0bs0Bu33iYKY ] (a capex of $31.1 billion left Meta with $784 million in free cash flow).
On Thursday, the stock closed at about $737, near where the Muse rally left it in September and below its August 2025 high of $790. Meta reports [ https://substack.com/redirect/2a2bab8e-e26d-4fff-9bdb-9093a3199505?j=eyJ1IjoiMndmMTJ4In0.oZAfFOSrevILB5Hnb24UxZa264EhpHj0bs0Bu33iYKY ] its third-quarter results next Wednesday. In those results, we’ll look for one number: how much Meta’s AI products earn, set against at least $130 billion of spending this year.
Industry Use Cases
Orbitworks and Loft Orbital Launch a Satellite That Uses AI to Spot Ships and Wildfires
The first of 50 planned satellites from Orbitworks and Loft Orbital reached orbit on October 1, and it uses AI to analyze its own images. About 20 models, running on two onboard NVIDIA Jetson computers, look for ships, wildfires, and floods, while Ministral 3, a small language model from Mistral, summarizes what the satellite has seen. Users get alerts within seconds instead of raw images hours later. The companies haven’t published any accuracy figures. Read more in this article from Loft Orbital [ https://substack.com/redirect/eaeb97a1-39a9-44b0-824b-71c4186dea0b?j=eyJ1IjoiMndmMTJ4In0.oZAfFOSrevILB5Hnb24UxZa264EhpHj0bs0Bu33iYKY ].
Google DeepMind Uses an AI Agent to Search for New Antibiotic Peptides
Google DeepMind researchers used Antigravity, Google’s AI coding tool, to look for antimicrobial peptides (small proteins that could help against drug-resistant bacteria). The AI searched protein databases, used a Google model that predicts a protein’s function from its sequence, then proposed how one candidate might work and drafted a lab plan. That candidate has no close match among the reviewed proteins in the Swiss-Prot database. It and several others are still being tested in the lab. Read more in this article from Google DeepMind [ https://substack.com/redirect/bbfa7c9e-67b8-458c-adac-e0aef3125ed4?j=eyJ1IjoiMndmMTJ4In0.oZAfFOSrevILB5Hnb24UxZa264EhpHj0bs0Bu33iYKY ].
Meta Says Its Muse AI Models Helped Researchers Write 10 Medical Studies in Four Weeks
Researchers from Stanford, Emory, and other institutions used a coding tool built on Meta’s Muse Spark 1.3 and Muse Wonder to study about 5 million de-identified health records. The AI helped them form hypotheses, write and run code, and draft 10 manuscripts, while the researchers made the scientific decisions. One study found a 7% one-year risk of a new heart-rhythm disorder in patients with endocarditis, against 4% in patients with bloodstream infections but no endocarditis. The studies only look back at past records, and outside reviewers flagged possible biases. Read more in this article from Meta [ https://substack.com/redirect/29fc7415-c0b6-4363-95dc-9186086c4af2?j=eyJ1IjoiMndmMTJ4In0.oZAfFOSrevILB5Hnb24UxZa264EhpHj0bs0Bu33iYKY ].

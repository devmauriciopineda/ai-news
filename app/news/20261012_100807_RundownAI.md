###### ATOMIC MACHINES

#### ⚙️ **[_Atomic Machines’ AI turns code into tiny machines_](https://www.atomicmachines.com/matter-compiler)**

View image: (https://media.beehiiv.com/cdn-cgi/image/fit=scale-down,format=auto,onerror=redirect,quality=80/uploads/asset/file/c6b3c537-62c4-4b5c-a34f-6f0b951f1012/matter.png?t=1791736184)
Caption: Image source: Atomic Machines

**The Rundown: **Atomic Machines, a California startup led by former Uber self-driving chief Jeff Holden, just [emerged](https://x.com/jeffholden/status/2108283819560472987?s=20) from stealth after six years with $250M raised and an AI-guided factory it says can turn digital designs into tiny, working machines.

**The details: **

* Holden says a 3D printer only alters an object’s shape, while his Matter Compiler builds a whole new device when the code changes, without needing retooling.

* Each production step is checked before a part moves on, with the process feeding the AI new data on which materials actually work, the company said.

* It focuses on gadgets with moving parts too small to see without a microscope, like tiny pumps, motors, and gears that chip factories can’t produce.

* Its first machine is PrimeSwitch, a fingertip-sized power switch for AI server farms that it says is faster than traditional breakers and more efficient than silicon.

**Why it matters: **Vibe coding lets people describe an app and get working software, and Atomic is now trying to do the same for physical hardware with “prompt-to-product manufacturing.” For now, it’s only building its own products with humans in the loop, but the idea is that “virtually anyone with a concept” could one day turn it into a real device.

###### THE RUNDOWN ROUNDTABLE

#### 💡 **_[The Rundown Roundtable: Our AI use cases](https://app.therundown.ai/community)_**

View image: (https://media.beehiiv.com/cdn-cgi/image/fit=scale-down,format=auto,onerror=redirect,quality=80/uploads/asset/file/afd087a8-7596-4329-9609-68331d0b549b/image-1791727920666.png?t=1791727948)

**The Rundown: **The Rundown Roundtable is a weekly feature where we poll members of The Rundown staff about how we use AI in our work and daily lives.

**Rishi, Growth: **I use Instinct, an AI assistant I text or send voice notes to over iMessage, to manage personal to-dos that used to live in my head or in a pile of iPhone alarms. Many tasks come up when I’m away from my desk, so I used to forget them. Alarms didn’t help much either: they went off once, I swiped them away, and the task was gone.

Now, I send Instinct a quick voice note. For example: “Remind me tomorrow at 10 AM to respond to book my dental appointment, and follow up until I say it's done.” Instinct turns the voice note into a clean reminder with a time attached. If I don’t reply “done,” it checks back in and keeps following up until I close out the task.

I use the same thread for recurring tasks, partner follow-ups, ad hoc tasks, and advertising to-dos, so everything I owe people lives in one place. Capturing a task takes a few seconds, which means I actually do it. The built-in follow-up keeps things from slipping just because I was busy when the reminder went off, and the workflow lives in iMessage, where I already am throughout the day.

**Shubham, Editor: **I’m a nerd for deals, so I built a Personal Credit Card Optimizer in ChatGPT. I wanted a simpler way to figure out which store and card would save me the most money on any item I want to purchase.

The workflow maintains a database of my credit and debit cards—recording their series, but not their exact numbers—along with their rewards, fees, and benefits. Whenever I want to buy something, I add a product link. It scans retailers for better prices, checks current bank offers against my specific cards, and compares instant discounts, cashback, reward points, and other benefits to determine where I should buy and which card will provide the most value.

It’s now an ongoing skill in my ChatGPT project. No more manually checking a dozen bank offers or discovering at checkout that my card isn’t eligible.

###### AI & SECURITY 

#### 🔒 **[_Microsoft CEO: Treat AI like it’s already been hacked_](https://x.com/satyanadella/status/2108931348857827686)**

View image: (https://media.beehiiv.com/cdn-cgi/image/fit=scale-down,format=auto,onerror=redirect,quality=80/uploads/asset/file/5e87f97e-ec58-4c3e-a735-009248257667/satya.png?t=1791731486)
Caption: Image source: Satya Nadella (@satyanadella on X) 

**The Rundown: **Microsoft CEO Satya Nadella [published](https://x.com/satyanadella/status/2108931348857827686) an essay on X urging companies to guard advanced AI models like a powerful employee who could slip up or get hacked, including an “emergency brake” and controls the AI can’t touch.

**The details: **

* Nadella argued that engineers could previously trace a misbehaving program or bug back to its code, but today’s AI models can’t always be explained that way.

* He wants AI’s permissions and activity logs kept where the AI can’t edit them and no model grading its own work, with a focus on auditable and verifiable systems.

* Nadella also said AI should have an “emergency brake” a human can use to shut down a system mid-task, saying firms should “assume a model is compromised.”

* He also wants models to show their reasoning in plain words, saying “Neuralese,” or AI thinking in its own unreadable code, isn’t an excuse to hide it.

**Why it matters: **Nadella’s ending says the most trustworthy system is the one “that enables us to trust the model the least,” and his calls in this essay feel like a direct response to [reports](https://www.therundown.ai/articles/meta-google-join-the-ai-launch-party) that OpenAI’s newest techniques can hide part of an AI’s reasoning from the humans monitoring it.
